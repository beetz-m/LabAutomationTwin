Challenge–Rescue Plan

First the experiment as the lab describes it, then the plan that gets executed to perform it, then its motion designators as Giskard problems, then how that plan runs. The 8 lab action designators span 72 hours of culture time. Each culture-time point becomes one trip of plate P17 out of the incubator, and each trip expands into lab tasks, manipulation steps, and finally the motions and device commands the robots and instruments execute. Nothing below was written by hand: it is the kernel's expansion of the lab plan, recorded in the audit trail.

  2 · The plan that gets executed
  
The structured reactive controller, in CRAM/RPL

  
Generated from the description above by rsa/ldl_to_cram.py, after rsa/ldl.py has checked it. It follows the CRAM structure: performing an action designator runs the plan of its action type. So the experiment plan is just the partial order of action designators, and everything reactive sits in the action plans and the top-level controller. Not executed here, since no Lisp CRAM is installed; the generator checks that the parentheses balance, that the :order constraints equal the derived order, and that no device or robot name appears.

  
2.1 The experiment plan: a partial order of action designators

  
Each :order is a causal link: the comment names the condition one step achieves and the next requires. The two challenges have no order between them, and neither do the three measurements.

  

  
2.2 The controller that runs it

  
It binds the entity designators once (a the designator is one object, passed by identity) and starts the process modules that fill the instrument roles. Its experiment-level repairs end in escalation. Its policies are the experiment's prevents and maintains: whenever monitors that never finish and evaporate with the plan they constrain. Its unwind-protect always returns the plate to its controlled environment.

  

  
2.3 The action plans: how each action designator is performed

  
One plan per action type, generated from that type's contract:
    ensure for what must be known (a shortfall goes to the meta model);
    wait-for for what a predecessor in the order supplies;
    achieve for what the plan may establish;
    the action's own prevents and maintains as policies (with the portion-scoped cross-dosing monitor);
    tending the instrument (insert the plate into its slot, start the process, take it out);
    the goal check;
    and the repairs that can still reach the goal, each with its verified guarantee.

  

  

  
Supporting definitions: the LDL macros (holds, prevent, maintain, scope, ensure, check-goal, def-action-plan), the failure conditions with the device faults behind them, the KPIs and the epistemic meta model

  3 · The motion level
  
Motion designators as Giskard problems: frames, constraints, objective

  
Each manipulation action designator (picking_up, transporting, placing, coupling_tool) refines into motion designators. Each motion designator is stated as a giskardpy motion statechart:
    reference frames (a root and a tip link of the robot, and a goal pose expressed in a seat frame of the SDT);
    motion constraints (Cartesian goals, straight-line motion, speed limits, keeping the plate level, collision avoidance and allowed contacts);
    monitors that end the motion;
    and the objective that Giskard's QP-based model-predictive controller optimizes at every control cycle.
  Generated by rsa/giskard_motions.py from the frames of the composed Cell 01 scene (rsa/motion_frames.py). The giskardpy classes and fields are checked against your CRAM checkout. Not executed here: a semantic_digital_twin World of Cell 01 is still missing.

3.2 The objective Giskard optimizes

  

    
minimize over q̇, q⃛ (per joint, k = 1..H) and the slacks ε_c of the soft constraints:

   Σ_k Σ_j  w_j,k · ( q̇_j,k² / q̇_j,max² )          joint velocities (dof weight 0.01), and jerks, normalised by their limits,
                                                  ramped over the horizon (horizon_weight_gain_scalar 0.1)
 + Σ_c  w_c / (v_ref,c² · (H − 2)) · ε_c²          slack of every soft constraint c, weighted by its priority w_c

subject to   joint position, velocity and jerk limits (box bounds from the robot's DOF limits)
             each constraint row: target − f(q) = Δt · Σ_k J_f q̇_k + Δt · ε_c   (integrated over the control horizon)
             collision rows:  distance ≥ buffer zone (0.05 m) − slack

    

    
The priorities are the slack weights. Keeping the plate level and the speed limits near the seats (2500) win over collision avoidance (50), which wins over reaching the goal frame (1), which wins over staying near the planned posture (0.1). Giskard satisfies the higher priority first whenever the constraints conflict. So it slows down, or holds still short of the goal, rather than tilting the plate or entering a buffer zone.

  

  
3.3 The reference frames and whether the robots can reach them

  
Goal frames in world coordinates, composed from the seat frames of the Cell 01 scene and the twin's grasp offsets. Each goal is checked with the robot's own kinematics (no simulator). R01 is checked with FR3 IK, a joint margin of at least 20°, the conditioning threshold and the station box collision model. R02 is checked with rail + UR5e IK (rail free) and a joint margin of at least 15°; its collision model is not available offline.
