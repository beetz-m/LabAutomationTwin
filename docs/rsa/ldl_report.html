Lab Designator Language

The lab states what an action must bring about, in terms of lab entities only. No robots, poses or device names. Every term is an entity description that the executive resolves against the semantic digital twin (SDT), down to a reference frame a controller can reach. Pick an example below and watch it ground in the twin on the right.

requiresholds, and is known to hold, when the action startsacceptance check, or a reason to establish it first
preventsno matching state or event occurs during the actionneeds a detector and a reaction
maintainsholds throughout the actionneeds a regulator and a recorded history
achievesholds at the end and persists, including the evidence recordpost-condition with an evidence path

2 · Anatomy

A designator is an entity description

The surface is HADL's s-expression; the reading is euROBIN's entity(X, δ). A determiner, a sort, an optional variable, and slots. A slot holds a value, a quantity, a variable or another designator.     4 · The whole experiment
    
The experiment as a partial order of action designators

    
The complete challenge–rescue experiment is eight lab action designators over eight entity designators. No order is written in the file. The checker (rsa/ldl.py) instantiates each step's category contract, then derives the order from causal links: a step's achieves-item entails another step's requires-item.     6 · Inside the instruments (v0.3)
    
Device processes, guarantees of repairs, and trust

    
Your next four answers: device processes have to be modelled and reasoned about, because they decide how far an experiment can be trusted; repairs must give guarantees for the final effects; the meta model needs expressive power now; and safety, reliability and auditability rank high among the KPIs, with costs. All of it is now in the language and checked by rsa/ldl.py and rsa/ldl_trust.py.

    
Process models, grounded in the component records

    
Each lab process (rsa/registry/lab/processes.ldl) names its phases, the phase after which it cannot be undone, and its faults. For each fault it says what the fault corrupts, which monitor detects it and how fully, whether a repeat meets the same cause, and which re-established condition removes it. Every name is checked against the Cell 01 component record of the device that fills the instrument role: faults against failure_modes, monitors against monitoring or risk_controls, evidence against the action contract, and "must not assume" against epistemic_state. Select a process.

    

    

    
Trust, step by step

    
For each step, these are the faults of its instrument's process that can corrupt the step's effects. Partly detected: a monitor sees only some instances. After the irreversible phase: the fault can be detected but not undone, so the result can only be rejected or quarantined. The evidence never discharges the "must not assume" items; they stay assumptions of the result.

    

    
Every repair states its guarantee, and the claim is checked

    
A handler claims goal, goal-or-safe-state, safe-state and/or contained.
    goal holds only if the fault is detected, occurs before the irreversible phase, and is either transient or removed by what the handler re-establishes.
    safe-state holds if the final branch escalates or abstains and the experiment can restore the plate to its controlled environment, with nothing committed and the evidence of the failed attempt kept.
    contained holds if a quarantining action with a containment goal is part of the repair.

    

    
The guarantees against the recorded runs

    
Every repair in the existing audit trails, mapped to the handler that covers it: did the claimed guarantee hold, and did the kernel repair the way the handler prescribes?

    

  


  

    7 · Reference frames
    
From a referent to a reachable frame

    
The lab text never contains a frame. Frames appear only after resolution, through the SDT's parent chain. Each node carries a local transform in metres (Z up), and composing them from the world down gives the target. The convention the plate seats state: the plate origin is its bottom centre, +X runs along the long axis, and the A1 corner is at (−X, +Y).

    

      
Parts decide frame parents
A well's frame is a child of its plate, and the plate's frame is a child of whatever seat holds it. The partonomy (hasDirectComponent) and the belief (plate_at) together decide the chain. A plate that moves takes its wells along with it.

      
Devices can move the parent
On L17 the plate rides on the XY stage. To bring a well under the objective, the stage travels by q = −(well offset + calibrated stage offset). The device realises the frame. The robot only reaches the load frame.

    

    

    
The chain opens in the Frames tab of the twin panel. Translations come from the composed Cell 01 scene and the twin's SBS plate model (wells 6.9 mm wide, 10.9 mm deep, so the bottom sits 3.45 mm above the plate base). Interface offsets (pipette +10 mm, imaging +5 mm) are as authored in the base SDT. The plate's yaw on the L12 and L17 seats is taken as 0, following the seat convention.

    9 · Found while building this page
    

      Grounding check · base SDT (read-only) · resolved in the overlay
      
The base SDT authors well frames in world coordinates

      
In the base scene, the wells of P17 carry world translations fixed at one design pose, and the plate root is the identity. The twin does not use them. It composes plate-relative frames from its SBS plate model, so the runs are unaffected.
